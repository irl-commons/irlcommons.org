How you can help

Streamers

  • Tell the makers what you want in their app. They depend on your feedback, and in our experience they're all happy and friendly. Each entry in Apps and services on How to IRL says where to reach its makers.
  • Share your setup: what you carry, where your stream breaks, and what you'd need. Know an app or guide that's missing? Add it to the open data.

Mods

  • Tell us which words confuse chat or the streamer, so the glossary can explain them, or add one yourself.
  • When a stream breaks, note what viewers saw: a frozen picture, a BRB screen, no sound.

Tech helpers

  • Field reports from real streams: what broke, what helped, and which app and service versions were involved.
  • Try How to IRL against setups you run, and say where they're wrong.
  • Add or confirm entries and guides in the open data.

Production companies and crews

  • Describe how you mix feeds from the studio, the desk and away from it, and where moving a stream from one place to another gets hard.
  • Tell us what you would need before you use open tools on a paid job.
  • Add the services and tools you use to the open data.

Developers

  • Open an issue or a discussion on GitHub.
  • If a fix belongs in Moblin, BELABOX, CeraLive or another project, send it there first. IRL Commons does the same.
  • Reports with real networks and real hardware help as much as code.

Stages of a spec

Each spec on Open standards is tagged with one of three stages.

Proposal
An idea for a spec, written down but not published yet. Anything can still change, including whether it goes ahead.
Draft
Published as v0, so people can try it and comment. Names and rules can still change, so expect to update what you build on it.
Standard
Released as v1. From then on, changes only add, so what works with it keeps working.

How a spec moves on

  • A proposal becomes a draft when it is published in its own public repository, with examples and a place to comment.
  • A draft becomes a standard once it has been tried on real devices, the comments have been answered, and it is released as v1.
