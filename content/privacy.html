What happens when you open a page

When you open a page, your browser sends the server your IP address, the address of the page and the usual technical details, such as browser type and language. The server needs these to send you the page, encrypted (HTTPS). The legal basis is my legitimate interest in providing the site (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

The server keeps none of it: it keeps no access log and writes no IP addresses to error logs. Once the page is delivered, the details are gone from the server.

The site runs on a server rented from netcup GmbH (Karlsruhe), in a data centre in Germany. The name irlcommons.org is resolved through Cloudflare (DNS only); the connection to the site itself doesn't pass through Cloudflare.

No cookies, no tracking

This site sets no cookies and doesn't count or track visitors. It embeds nothing from other sites. The fonts (Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and IBM Plex Mono) are on the same server; your browser loads nothing from Google or anyone else.

Your browser keeps files in its cache. For one day, it keeps a record that it should open the site only over an encrypted connection (HSTS). It also keeps a colour theme you pick until you clear this site's data. The theme button switches between dark and light. The Beta badge stores no data.

Links to other sites

This site links to GitHub and to the projects and services it names. Those sites load only when you click a link, and your browser doesn't tell them which page you came from. From then on, that site's own privacy policy applies.

GitHub, where contributions happen, is run by GitHub, Inc. in the USA under its own rules: GitHub's privacy statement.

The site also links to the project's server on Discord. Nothing loads from Discord until you click the link. On Discord, Discord's privacy policy applies.

If you write to me

Mail to hello@irlcommons.org goes through Cloudflare's Email Routing, which forwards it to my inbox. Cloudflare handles the message to deliver it and doesn't keep it as a mailbox. Its documentation says it doesn't store or access the emails it forwards. Cloudflare does keep a record of each message in an activity log that I can see in my Cloudflare account: sender, recipient, subject, time and whether it was forwarded.

If you email or call me, I use your details only to answer, and delete them once they're no longer needed for that (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). What you write on GitHub is visible there under GitHub's rules.

Nothing is passed on

I don't pass data on to anyone, sell it or analyse it.
