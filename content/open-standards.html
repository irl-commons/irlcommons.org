Standards, drafts and proposals

irl-commons Draft
Shared facts
Words, quick checks, a list of apps and services, guides and standards, written in plain language. Anyone can reuse them with credit. How to IRL's glossary, app list and guides come from here.
Published, v0 · github.com/irl-commons
stream-status Draft
What's wrong, and are you still live?
Each part reports its problems in the same short shape: what is wrong, where, how much it matters to viewers, and whether the stream still reaches the receiver. A part that can't see something says "unknown", never "fine".
Published, v0 · No app uses it yet · github.com/irl-commons/stream-status
stream-session Draft
Which show, who's in it, who decides?
The devices in one show agree on which device is in charge. So a stream can move from your PC to your phone to your backpack, and a friend or a guest can join. Each person chooses what the others see. Anyone allowed can hide the picture at once.
Published, v0 · No app uses it yet · github.com/irl-commons/stream-session
stream-components Proposal
Who's around, who trusts whom, what goes where?
How your phone, backpack, OBS and other apps would find each other, pair once with a QR code, and show where each picture and sound goes. It is meant to work on one Wi-Fi or hotspot, and across the internet through an optional server.
Designed, not published yet

Comments wanted

These are drafts, so now is the easiest time to change them. Comments from makers and integrators help most.

If you make a streaming app, an encoder box, a relay or receiver, a hub, a scene switcher, a remote app or a help page: what would stop you from using one of these?

  • Something your product needs to say that a draft can't say.
  • A rule that doesn't fit how your product works.
  • Too much work for what you would get back.

One line on why you wouldn't use it is as useful as a long review. Doubts about the whole idea are welcome too.

Where to write

  • On GitHub: each repository has a "Feedback from makers and integrators" discussion: irl-commons, stream-status, stream-session.
  • By email: hello@irlcommons.org, if you'd rather not post in public.
  • On Discord: the project's Discord server.

Try one first

  • Use the irl-commons data in your app or site.
  • Send your first stream-status snapshot.
  • Join a stream-session show.

Each guide takes about ten minutes, and each repository has worked examples and an FAQ.

Built on earlier work

NOALBS
The open-source scene switcher many IRL streamers run. It reads the stats of the common receivers, and it is the closest thing to a shared status format in IRL streaming today. stream-status and stream-session are meant to sit next to it.
IRLToolkit
Its ingest sharing for cloud OBS, where both sides agree and access runs out by itself by default, shaped how stream-session lets guests in.
OBS Studio
Its broadcast performance metrics, carried inside the video, are the model for stream-status's in-band form.
Broadcast and monitoring
AMWA NMOS, Prometheus Alertmanager, the IETF alarm model, Kubernetes conditions, Nagios, OpenTelemetry and CloudEvents.
Coordination and sharing
Raft, Chubby, etcd and NATS leases, fencing tokens, DNS-SD, OAuth scopes, Biscuit and macaroons, LiveKit, Matrix and W3C Trace Context.
